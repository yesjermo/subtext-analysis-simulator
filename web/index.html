How to get your chats

Each app's own download. Open the file here; it never leaves this device.
Share from any appquickest, on your phone
Android: install Subtext (Chrome → ⋮ → Install app) and open it once. Then select a message, a screenshot or WhatsApp's Export chat file in any app → Share → Subtext. iPhone: add it to your home screen (Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen), copy a message or a screenshot, and tap Paste from clipboard. For a share-menu button, set up the Decode with Subtext shortcut.
Instagramalso Facebook Messenger
Settings → Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Download your information. Pick Messages and format JSON. When it arrives, unzip it and open the person's folder in your_instagram_activity/messages/inbox/ with Open an export folder, or just its message_1.json.
TikTok
Profile → Settings and privacy → Account → Download your data. Choose JSON and include direct messages. Open user_data_tiktok.json; every DM thread inside is listed to pick from.
Snapchat
accounts.snapchat.com → My Data. Tick Chat History and turn on Export JSON files. Open json/chat_history.json. Snapchat only keeps chats that were saved or not yet opened.
WhatsApp
Open the chat → their name → Export chat → Without media. Open the .txt (unzip it first if it arrives as a .zip).
Texts on Android
Install SMS Backup & Restore, back up your messages, and open the .xml it makes. Every contact is listed to pick from.
iMessageand SMS on iPhone
Apple has no export. On a Mac with Messages in iCloud, run python3 get_chat.py imessage --list, then --chat <number> --them <name> --out chat.csv, and open the .csv here. No Mac: use screenshots.
Hinge, Tinder, Bumble, Discord
Their downloads only include your messages, so use screenshots: take them oldest first, with their bubbles on the left and yours on the right, and open them all at once. Check the words it read before decoding.
